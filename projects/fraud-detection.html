Credit Card Fraud Detection

Catching 492 frauds in 284,807 transactions without drowning in false positives.

Pythonimbalanced-learnSMOTEXGBoostPR-AUC

Overview

A fraud-detection classifier on a real anonymized credit-card transaction dataset, where fraud accounts for just 0.172% of all transactions.

Problem

With only 492 frauds among 284,807 transactions, a model that never predicts fraud already scores 99.8% accuracy, so accuracy is actively misleading and the real challenge is precision and recall on the rare class, evaluated here with Precision-Recall AUC (Average Precision).

Approach

  1. Establish a no-resampling baselineScore Logistic Regression, Linear Discriminant Analysis, and Naive Bayes directly on the raw class distribution, using the 28 PCA-transformed features plus Time and Amount.
  2. Under-sample the majority classCompare Random Undersampling and NearMiss, both of which discard normal transactions to rebalance the training set.
  3. Over-sample the minority classCompare Random Oversampling and SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling), both of which synthesize additional fraud examples instead.
  4. Scale up to ensembles on the best resampling strategyTrain Random Forest and XGBoost on SMOTE-resampled data, the combination most likely to handle the rare class well.

Random Forest on SMOTE-resampled data is the clear winner: 87.5% average precision with 85.3% precision and 82.7% recall on the fraud class, essentially the best of both worlds. Undersampling with NearMiss is a trap for linear models specifically, Logistic Regression collapses to 0.218 PR-AUC, while the same resampling helps SVM reach 0.768, showing resampling strategy and model choice interact and can't be tuned independently.
