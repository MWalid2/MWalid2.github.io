Telecom Customer Churn Prediction

Predicting which subscribers cancel, from call and account activity alone.

PythonRandom ForestXGBoostROC-AUCFeature Engineering

Overview

A churn classifier on the Orange Telecom dataset, predicting which of 2,666 customers cancel their subscription based on account and usage activity.

Problem

Churn is a minority outcome (about 14.5% of customers churn), and several numeric-looking columns (area code, customer service calls) actually behave as categorical, so getting the feature types right matters as much as the model choice.

Approach

  1. Separate true numeric from pseudo-categorical featuresTreat area code, international/voicemail plan, and customer service calls as categorical despite their numeric encoding, keeping call minutes/charges and account length as the real numeric features.
  2. Handle the 14.5% imbalance with class weightingUse class-weighted models rather than resampling, appropriate at this less extreme imbalance ratio.
  3. Compare five classifiersLogistic Regression, SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost, all class-weighted, trained on an 80/20 split.
  4. Evaluate beyond accuracyScore every model on confusion matrix, precision, recall, ROC-AUC, and Average Precision.
  5. Validate on a second, untouched datasetRun the same evaluation on the separate churn-20 holdout file, a dataset the models never saw during training or tuning.

Tree ensembles dominate the linear models here by a wide margin: Random Forest and XGBoost both clear 0.86 ROC-AUC against roughly 0.70–0.71 for Logistic Regression and SVM. XGBoost reaches the highest precision (93.4%, few false alarms) while Random Forest edges it on recall (75.6%) and ties it on Average Precision, a genuine precision/recall tradeoff between the two best models rather than one dominating outright.
