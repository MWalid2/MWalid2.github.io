Stochastic Diffusion & Convection Simulation

Monte Carlo particle diffusion through fractal boundaries, checked against the analytical solution.

MATLABPythonMonte Carlo SimulationPDEsFractal Geometry

Overview

A transport-phenomena study for biomedical engineering coursework, comparing stochastic (Monte Carlo) and theoretical models of particle diffusion and convection across irregular and fractal boundaries.

Problem

Real biological surfaces, alveolar membranes, vascular walls, are irregular or fractal-like, not the smooth boundaries most textbook diffusion solutions assume. It matters whether a stochastic particle simulation actually agrees with the theoretical concentration field once the boundary gets complicated.

Approach

  1. Simulate diffusion as a random walkModel air particles diffusing stochastically within a domain bounded by a Koch curve, a canonical fractal, generating a Monte Carlo concentration field over many simulated particles.
  2. Derive the analytical comparisonSolve the theoretical diffusion equation for the same domain and boundary conditions, including a Robin boundary-condition variant, to get a ground-truth concentration field to compare against.
  3. Quantify the stochastic-vs-theoretical errorProduce heatmaps of the stochastic mean concentration, the theoretical concentration, and their difference, plus a cross-sectional error plot along a single axis.
  4. Run the convection studyCompare flow rate under deformed vs. undeformed boundary geometries, isolating how geometric irregularity alone changes the convective transport rate.
  5. Animate the processProduce an animation of the stochastic diffusion simulation for visual inspection of the particle dynamics over time.
