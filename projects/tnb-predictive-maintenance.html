Predictive Maintenance

Predictive Maintenance Pipeline (TNB)

Predicting next-cycle defect occurrence in 33 kV distribution switchgear from multi-state utility data.

PythonPyTorchscikit-learnXGBoostPandasMulti-task Learning

Overview

An end-to-end predictive maintenance pipeline built during a Data Science Research Intern role at Universiti Tenaga Nasional (UNITEN), Malaysia, using multi-state utility data from Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) to predict next-cycle recorded defect occurrence in 33 kV distribution switchgear.

Problem

Distribution utilities record switchgear defects (hotspot, tracking, corona, mechanical vibration) and forced outages across thousands of functional locations and regional datasets that don't share a common key. Multi-state records like these are noisy and unevenly captured, and a maintenance model built on a badly integrated dataset will fail silently in production, exactly the risk this benchmark was built to catch before it reaches the grid.

Approach

  1. Integrate multi-state utility dataMerge QR03 defect/event records with LGBNet forced-outage records across 11 Malaysian regional datasets (Kuala Lumpur, Johor, Kelantan, Melaka, and others), matching functional locations across sources that don't share a common key.
  2. Engineer a monthly panelBuild one row per functional location per active month, with defect and outage counts per channel plus recency features (months since the last event on each channel), yielding a 62,744-row, 12-month sequence × 71-feature tabular panel.
  3. Frame it as multi-task predictionPredict 5 defect/outage channels (hotspot, tracking, corona, mechanical vibration, outage) at 3 forecast horizons (1, 3, and 6 months ahead), 15 binary “will this recur” targets sharing one input panel.
  4. Benchmark classical MLGrid-search Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, and XGBoost under both class-weighting and SMOTE (8 configurations), scored on PR-AUC (Average Precision) with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.
  5. Benchmark deep architecturesCompare a class-weighted perceptron, a tuned DNN, and sequence models (GRU, LSTM, Deep LSTM, attention-LSTM) plus a hybrid tabular+sequence model, all trained on identical splits and scored on the same held-out test set.

Honest finding: added architectural complexity didn't pay off here, a simple class-weighted perceptron (0.565 AP) edged out every recurrent architecture, including a purpose-built hybrid tabular+sequence model. Across the full 15-task multi-task benchmark (5 defect channels × 3 horizons), the mean PR-AUC across tasks was close for every architecture (Attention LSTM 0.127, GRU 0.126, Random Forest 0.121, LSTM 0.117), with GRU winning outright on 5 of 15 tasks and Random Forest and Attention LSTM tying for second at 4 wins each, evidence that no single architecture dominates uniformly across defect types and horizons.
