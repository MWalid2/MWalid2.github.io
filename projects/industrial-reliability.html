Reliability Engineering

Industrial Reliability & Maintenance Optimization

Finding the maintenance interval that minimizes cost across a 1,000-unit server fleet.

PythonSciPyNumPyMatplotlibWeibull AnalysisMarkov Chains

Overview

Statistical reliability study modeling equipment failure across a fleet of 1,000 industrial servers, translating raw time-to-failure data into a maintenance policy with a quantified cost impact.

Problem

Organizations running large equipment fleets face a tradeoff between corrective maintenance (repair after failure, expensive and disruptive) and preventive maintenance (replace on a schedule, wasteful if too frequent). Without a model of the failure distribution, that schedule is a guess.

Approach

  1. Fit the failure distributionFit a two-parameter Weibull distribution to the fleet's time-to-failure data, using both ordinary least squares on the linearized CDF and maximum likelihood estimation, to recover the shape (β) and scale (η) parameters.
  2. Validate the fitRun chi-squared and Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit tests against the fitted Weibull distribution before trusting it for any downstream decision.
  3. Optimize the replacement intervalApply Barlow-Proschan cost-rate optimization: numerically root-find the replacement age that minimizes long-run cost per unit time, trading preventive-replacement cost against the expected cost of running to failure.
  4. Model fleet availabilityExtend the single-unit result to the whole fleet with a Markov chain / Chapman-Kolmogorov up-down state model, to back out the repair rate needed for a target availability level.

Results

MetricValue
Shape parameter β2.97 (95% CI 2.83–3.12)
Scale parameter η3,958 h
Optimal preventive replacement interval1,485 h (≈62 days)
Cost reduction vs. corrective-only maintenance63.6%
Repair rate μ* for 90% availability2.57 / 1,000 h

The Weibull shape parameter (β ≈ 2.97, close to 3) indicates classic wear-out failures rather than random early-life failures, which is exactly the regime where a scheduled preventive-replacement policy pays off: replacing units at 1,485 hours instead of waiting for failures cuts the fleet's long-run maintenance cost by nearly two-thirds.
