Reliability Engineering

⚙️ Industrial Reliability & Maintenance Optimization

Finding the maintenance interval that minimizes cost across a 1,000-unit server fleet.

PythonSciPyNumPyMatplotlibWeibull AnalysisMarkov Chains

Overview

Statistical reliability study modeling equipment failure across a fleet of 1,000 industrial servers, translating raw time-to-failure data into a maintenance policy with a quantified cost impact.

Problem

Organizations running large equipment fleets face a tradeoff between corrective maintenance (repair after failure, expensive and disruptive) and preventive maintenance (replace on a schedule, wasteful if too frequent). Without a model of the failure distribution, that schedule is a guess.

Approach

Fitted a two-parameter Weibull distribution to failure data using both ordinary least squares and maximum likelihood estimation, validated the fit with chi-squared and Kolmogorov-Smirnov goodness-of-fit tests, then applied Barlow-Proschan cost-rate optimization (numerical root-finding on the renewal-reward cost function) to locate the replacement interval that minimizes long-run cost per unit time. Extended the analysis with a Markov chain model of the fleet's up/down state to estimate the repair rate needed for a target availability.

Results

  • Weibull shape parameter β ≈ 2.97 (95% CI 2.83-3.12), indicating wear-out failures
  • Scale parameter η ≈ 3,958 hours
  • Optimal preventive replacement interval: 1,485 hours (about 62 days)
  • 63.6% cost reduction versus corrective-only maintenance
  • Repair rate μ* ≈ 2.57 per 1,000 hours needed for 90% availability
