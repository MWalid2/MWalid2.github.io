German Credit Risk Scoring

Scoring loan applicants as good or bad credit risk against an imbalanced baseline.

Pythonscikit-learnXGBoostFeature EncodingPCA

Overview

A credit-risk classifier built on the classic German Credit dataset: 1,000 loan applicants labeled as good or bad credit risk.

Problem

The dataset is imbalanced (700 good vs. 300 bad, a 70/30 split), so a model that always predicts “good” already scores 70% accuracy, meaning accuracy alone can't be trusted to judge performance; AUC-ROC is used throughout instead.

Approach

  1. Engineer features under uncertaintyBucket age, credit amount, and duration into bins, and compare ordinal vs. target encoding for categoricals, with missing-value handling for the savings and checking-account columns (183 and 394 missing respectively).
  2. Cross-validate four classifiers5-fold stratified cross-validation comparing Logistic Regression, SVM, Decision Tree, and XGBoost on AUC-ROC, each inside a full preprocessing pipeline (Robust/Standard/MinMax scaling).
  3. Tune the strongest pipelineGrid search over encoding, scaling, and Logistic Regression hyperparameters together, not just the model.
  4. Test PCA dimensionality reductionCompress the encoded feature set to 7 principal components (90% explained variance) and compare the resulting pipeline against the full-feature one.

Results

5-fold cross-validated AUC-ROC (mean ± std):

ModelAUC-ROC (5-fold CV)
SVM0.732 ± 0.044
XGBoost0.730 ± 0.017
Logistic Regression0.684 ± 0.049
Decision Tree0.609 ± 0.032

Held-out test set, tuned Logistic Regression pipeline:

PipelineBest CV AUCTest AUC
Grid-searched Logistic Regression0.704 (CV)0.629 (test)
+ PCA (7 components, 90% var.)—0.631 (test)
Honest finding: the gap between 0.704 cross-validated AUC and 0.629 on the untouched test set, on a dataset of only 1,000 applicants, is a real reminder that small-data cross-validation scores carry meaningful variance. PCA compression didn't close that gap: the 7-component pipeline scored almost identically to the full-feature model, suggesting the signal here isn't concentrated in a few correlated directions.
