Citrus Production Forecasting

Forecasting citrus yield per hectare with CatBoost, across five Moroccan growing regions.

PythonCatBoostTime SeriesFeature EngineeringPandas

Overview

A yield-forecasting pipeline for citrus (“agrumes”) production across five Moroccan regions (Gharb, Tadla, Haouz, Souss, Oriental), predicting tonnage per hectare per parcel from historical harvest records joined against climate, irrigation, and fertilization data, using a CatBoost gradient-boosted model.

Problem

Agricultural yield depends on a web of interacting factors, rainfall, irrigation schedule, fertilizer (NPK) application, tree age, variety, and region-specific soil and climate, spread across five separate data sources with no shared key and hundreds of parcels missing records in at least one source. Turning that into a usable per-parcel yield forecast means solving the data integration problem before the modeling problem even starts, and getting the validation methodology right so the reported accuracy is actually trustworthy.

Approach

  1. Build a common parcel keyConstruct one identifier per variety, parcel number, campaign (growing season), and region, then join tonnage, irrigation, fertilizer, and parcel-registry records on it, keeping only the 701 training and 188 test parcels present in all four sources.
  2. Engineer yield and agronomic featuresFit a sigmoid tree-age yield curve and an per-hectare production cap from domain assumptions, then derive cumulative and rolling-window features for irrigation, N/P/K fertilizer, rainfall, evapotranspiration, and degree-days, plus cyclical month encoding and mapped variety/soil-type groups.
  3. Train a CatBoost regressorGradient-boosted trees (CatBoost, native categorical handling for region, farm, rootstock, and variety) trained directly on monthly tonnage, first validated with campaign 23/24 held out.
  4. Catch a validation leak, and fix itRefactor into a two-stage model: predict total tonnage/hectare per parcel per campaign with a proper GroupShuffleSplit (no parcel's data appears in both train and evaluation), then disaggregate into monthly tonnage using an empirical seasonal harvest-share pattern learned per calendar month.
  5. Tune tree depthSweep CatBoost depth from 5 to 9 with early stopping, selecting the depth/iteration combination with the best validation R².

Honest finding: the naive campaign-holdout split reported R² = 0.905, but the same parcel's history can appear in both the training years and the held-out campaign, letting the model partly memorize per-parcel effects instead of learning general agronomic relationships. Switching to a GroupShuffleSplit that keeps every parcel entirely in either train or evaluation, never both, drops the honest R² to about 0.62 (depth 6 best). The gap between the two numbers is itself the useful result: it quantifies how much of the first model's apparent accuracy was leakage rather than signal.

In the leakage-safe model, farm identity and tree age are the strongest predictors (feature importance: Code Ferme 18.3, age_plantation_annees 16.4, cumulative regional rainfall 16.3, cumulative irrigation 12.0, rootstock 10.0), ahead of any single fertilizer nutrient. The learned monthly harvest-share pattern used to disaggregate the campaign-level forecast peaks early in the season (65.9% of the annual pattern-weight in month 1, tapering to 2.1% by month 10), consistent with citrus harvest timing.
